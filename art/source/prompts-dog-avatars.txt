DOG AVATARS — ten side profiles for the profile picker
=======================================================================

Ten 1:1 dog portraits, one flat shared background, cropped the way the
app icon was cropped: one subject, big, centred, nothing that matters
near the edge.

  OUT ->  art/source/avatars/dog-01.png  …  dog-10.png   (1:1, 2048²)
  IN  ->  img/avatars/dog-01.jpg          …  dog-10.jpg   (400²)


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THESE TEN
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The brief was "the ten most common dog types". Straight popularity is
the wrong list for a picker, and it is worth saying why before you spend
ten generations on it.

The AKC's most-registered breeds run roughly: French Bulldog, Labrador,
Golden Retriever, German Shepherd, Poodle, Dachshund, English Bulldog,
Beagle, Rottweiler, German Shorthaired Pointer. Four of those are near-
duplicates of each other *as silhouettes* — an English Bulldog reads as
a French Bulldog at 40px, a Rottweiler reads as a black-and-tan German
Shepherd, a Shorthaired Pointer reads as a Labrador. Spending three of
ten slots on those leaves whole categories of real dog with nothing to
pick.

It also misses the two largest populations in actual homes. Registration
counts purebreds; roughly half of US dogs are mixed, and "pit bull type"
is not one AKC breed so it does not appear at all despite being one of
the most common dogs in the country.

So these ten are chosen for **coverage of shape** — the question a person
is really answering is "which of these looks most like my dog", not
"which breed is mine". Between them they cover short-coat large,
long-coat large, erect-ear working, flat-faced small, curly, elongated,
small hound, medium herding mix, blocky short-coat, and toy fluffy.
Almost any real dog lands near one.

  01  Labrador Retriever, black     short coat, drop ears, broad head
  02  Golden Retriever              long feathered coat, warm gold
  03  German Shepherd               erect ears, black-and-tan saddle
  04  French Bulldog                flat face, bat ears, compact
  05  Poodle / doodle mix           dense curly coat, apricot
  06  Dachshund, smooth red         long body, short legs, long muzzle
  07  Beagle                        small tricolour hound, long ears
  08  Border Collie type            semi-erect ears, black and white
  09  Pit bull type / Staffordshire blocky head, rose ears, fawn
  10  Shih Tzu type                 toy, long silky coat, short muzzle

01 is the anchor and is deliberately Lucy's build, so the set ties to
the art already in the app and a black-Lab household picks the dog they
already know.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO RUN IT — THIS PART MATTERS MORE THAN THE PROMPTS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The icon and splash file tells you to start a fresh conversation for
each image. **Do the opposite here, with one twist.** Those two images
had nothing to do with each other. These ten have to look like one set,
and ten independent generations will come back at ten different head
sizes on ten different creams.

  1. Generate 01 on its own. Iterate until it is right — the background
     cream, the crop, the head size in frame. Everything else inherits
     from it, so time spent here is spent ten times.

  2. For 02 through 10: START A NEW CONVERSATION, and ATTACH THE
     FINISHED 01 as a reference. Say "match this exactly: background
     colour, crop, head size in frame, lighting, rendering."

New conversation each time stops breed detail from the previous dog
bleeding into the next one — ask for a Beagle right after a Dachshund in
one thread and it will give you a long-backed Beagle. Attaching 01 each
time is what keeps the set together without that drift.

Check 02–10 against 01 side by side at 40px, not at full size. That is
the size this is actually used at, and it is where a head half a size
too big is obvious and a whisker count is not.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------
THE CROP, WHICH IS THE SAME PROBLEM THE ICON HAD
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The icon note says: three sizes get cut from it, Android keeps only the
middle 80%, so one subject, big, centred, nothing important near the
edges. Same here, for a different reason — **the avatar is a circle.**

A circle inscribed in a square throws away the corners: about 21% of the
image is never seen. So the head has to sit inside a centred circle of
roughly 80% of the width, and the corners have to be nothing but
background. An ear tip or a muzzle in the corner is an ear tip or a
muzzle that gets sliced off.

That is also why the framing below is head-and-upper-chest rather than
the whole dog. A full-body side profile at 40px in a circle is a dark
smudge, which is exactly the failure the icon note records for the full
scene at 180px.

All ten face **right**. One direction or the set looks like a crowd
arguing; right because in the person rows the avatar sits to the left of
the name, so the dog looks into the text rather than off the edge.

No collar on any of them. A purple collar would say "this is Lucy" on
ten different dogs, and any other colour introduces a second brand
colour to keep consistent across ten generations. Bare neck is one less
thing to get wrong. If you want the collar, add it to 01 before you use
it as the anchor and it will carry through the rest.

SHARED BLOCK — paste this above every prompt, all ten times
=======================================================================

STYLE — Contemporary dog-training instruction manual illustration.
Simplified vector-like forms, crisp edges, restrained detail. Mostly
flat colour with subtle dimensional shading. Muted green, teal, blue and
charcoal palette. Warm, approachable, premium, adult. No photorealism,
no painterly texture, no childish cartoon treatment, no sketch lines.

FRAMING — Square 1:1. Strict side profile, facing right. Head, neck and
upper chest only; no legs, no body below the shoulder. The near eye is
visible and open, ears fully within frame, muzzle pointing right and
stopping well short of the right edge.

PLACEMENT — The head is centred and occupies about 70% of the frame
height. Everything that matters sits inside a centred circle covering
80% of the width, because this image is masked to a circle: all four
corners must be plain background and nothing else. Generous even margin
on every side. No part of the dog touches or crosses any edge.

BACKGROUND — One completely flat, uniform warm cream, hex #F6E6D0, edge
to edge. No gradient, no vignette, no texture, no cast shadow, no
ground, no horizon, no props, no scenery, no border, no frame, no circle
drawn in the image.

LIGHTING — Soft, even, from the upper left. Gentle dimensional shading
to give the head form. No harsh shadows, no rim light, no highlights
that read as gloss.

The dog wears no collar, no harness, no tag, no bandana.

No text, lettering, labels, watermarks, logos or UI of any kind.

AFTER GENERATION
=======================================================================

Save each as art/source/avatars/dog-NN.png, then:

  mkdir -p "img/avatars" && cd "/Users/ahickey/dev/claude-local/Lucy Learns" && for f in art/source/avatars/dog-*.png; do sips -Z 400 -s format jpeg -s formatOptions 80 "$f" --out "img/avatars/$(basename "$f" .png).jpg"; done

400² because the existing brand mark `lucy-portrait` is 400² and this is
used no larger. Ten at ~40 KB is ~400 KB, which the service worker
precaches with everything else — worth checking against the current
payload before adding a second set at a larger size.

Then check all ten together, circle-masked, at 40px. That is the only
review that counts.
