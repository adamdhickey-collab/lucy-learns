# Scene prompts — new trainer and new guest

Eleven scenes still to redraw. The twelve images delivered on 19 August cover the
guest slots and are already app-ready at 1448×1086; these are the ones nothing in
either delivery depicts correctly.

Paste **Block A** and **Block B** ahead of each scene prompt. They are what keeps
forty illustrations looking like one set rather than forty separate pictures, so
they go in every time, unchanged, even when it feels repetitive.

The line under each prompt marked **Must be true** is not styling. Each of these
images sits on a step that teaches something, and the picture is the instruction —
a handler leaning over the dog, or a taut leash where the point is a slack one,
teaches the opposite of what the words beside it say.

---

## Block A — style

> Painted digital illustration, warm and calm: soft airbrushed shading, clean
> confident linework, matte finish. Not photorealistic, not cartoonish, no heavy
> outlines, no harsh shadows, no lens blur or bokeh. Landscape 4:3, 1448×1086.
>
> Interior entryway of an ordinary suburban home: warm cream-peach walls, white
> baseboards, honey-toned wood floor, a charcoal-grey panelled front door with a
> four-pane window in its upper half, a coir doormat inside the threshold, a
> rectangular grey dog bed with a soft rolled edge. Even, soft daylight from the
> door. Muted palette — olive green, dusty blue, warm cream, charcoal, honey wood.
> Plenty of empty floor; the room is calm and uncluttered.

## Block B — the cast

> **THE HANDLER** — a woman in her early forties of East Asian descent, dark brown
> hair in a low ponytail with a few loose strands at the temple, a warm olive-green
> pullover hoodie, dark navy jeans, grey trainers, and a mustard-yellow treat pouch
> clipped at her right hip. Calm and unhurried. She never looms over the dog: she
> crouches or kneels to the dog's level, or stands straight and relaxed.
>
> **THE GUEST** — a man in his late forties, clear-framed glasses, a **black
> baseball cap**, a **medium-blue zip-up hoodie** over a white tee, dark navy
> trousers, dark shoes. Friendly, relaxed, unhurried. **He must wear the blue
> hoodie and black cap in every image. Do not draw him in a plaid or checked
> shirt** — that was an earlier version of this character and is being replaced
> everywhere.
>
> **LUCY** — a black Labrador mix with a glossy black coat, a small white flash on
> her chest, white tips on her toes, and a flat **purple collar with a round blue
> tag**. Medium-large and athletic, with a soft, gentle expression. Never anxious,
> never cowering.

## The eleven scenes

### 1. door-sound-01-setup — getting set up
> The handler stands relaxed beside Lucy a few feet back from the closed front
> door. Lucy sits at her left side, facing the door. The leash runs from Lucy's
> collar down to the floor and **passes under the handler's shoe**, which is what
> leaves both of the handler's hands free and empty at her sides. Wide enough to
> show the whole leash run and the closed door.

**Must be true:** the leash is visibly under her shoe, and both her hands are empty. That is the entire point of the step.

### 2. door-sound-05-settle — the pause between repetitions
> A quiet in-between moment. Lucy stands loose and relaxed beside the standing
> handler on a slack leash that hangs in a soft curve to the floor. Neither is
> working: the handler is not cueing, pointing, or offering food, and Lucy is not
> being asked for anything. The closed front door is a few feet away. Calm, ordinary,
> almost uneventful.

**Must be true:** the leash hangs slack, and nobody is doing anything. It is a picture of nothing being asked.

### 3. door-stay-03-cross — walking away from the bed
> Lucy lies on her bed in the foreground with her head up and her eyes following
> the handler, who is **mid-stride walking away from her toward the closed front
> door**, seen from behind or three-quarters from behind. Clear floor between the
> bed and the door so the distance reads. Lucy is settled and staying, not rising.

**Must be true:** the handler is clearly in motion and moving away, and Lucy is staying down.

### 4. door-stay-03-pretend — talking to nobody
> The handler stands in the **open** front doorway with her back three-quarters to
> us, talking out through it — mouth slightly open, one hand gesturing lightly — to
> a **completely empty porch**. Daylight fills the open doorway. Lucy stays lying on
> her bed across the room, head up, watching. **No other person anywhere in frame.**

**Must be true:** the porch is empty. She is rehearsing the conversation, and a visitor in the doorway makes it a different exercise.

### 5. door-stay-03-conversation — the longer conversation
> The handler **leans her shoulder against the edge of the open front door**, relaxed
> and settled in, mid-conversation with an **empty porch** — a longer, more casual
> pose than someone who has just opened the door. Lucy stays settled on her bed
> across the room, relaxed, head down or resting. **No other person in frame.**

**Must be true:** she is leaning and settled — this scene is about a *long* stay, so her posture has to read as unhurried. Empty porch again.

### 6. door-stay-04-pay — paying for the stay
> The handler **kneels on one knee** beside Lucy's bed and feeds her a treat from a
> flat open palm. Lucy stays lying down on the bed and takes the food without
> getting up — her body stays on the bed. Close, warm, quiet. The closed front door
> is visible behind them.

**Must be true:** Lucy stays down while eating. If she is standing up to take the treat, the picture rewards the wrong thing.

### 7. door-place-cover — settled on the bed
> Lucy lies settled and comfortable on her bed, relaxed, with the handler crouched
> beside her at her level, one hand resting gently on the dog's shoulder. They are
> **well back from the closed front door**, which sits in the background. Warm and
> companionable, the calm end-state of the exercise.

**Must be true:** distance between them and the door. This is the calm they are working toward, not a doorway drill.

### 8. door-place-03-send — sending her to the bed
> Lucy is **mid-step, stepping up onto her bed** — one or two paws on it, weight
> shifting forward. The handler stands **behind and slightly to one side**, arm
> extended, pointing to the bed. The closed front door is nearby. A moment of
> motion, not a finished position.

**Must be true:** Lucy is arriving on the bed, not already lying on it, and the handler is pointing from behind her rather than luring her from the front.

### 9. door-greet-05-reward — paying her while a guest waits
> The handler **crouches** beside Lucy's bed and feeds her a treat from a flat palm.
> Lucy stays lying on the bed. At the **open front door in the background stands the
> guest** — black cap, blue hoodie — waiting patiently just outside the threshold,
> not entering, not looking at the dog. Daylight behind him.

**Must be true:** the guest waits outside and ignores Lucy; the reward happens while she holds the bed with him in view.

### 10. plan-fourpaws — four on the floor
> Lucy stands square with **all four paws on the floor**, looking up at a person who
> stands straight with **both hands held together at their waist** — not reaching,
> not petting, not holding food. A plain, softly lit interior. The person can be
> cropped at the shoulders or shown fully; their calm, closed hands are the subject
> as much as the dog is.

**Must be true:** all four paws down, and the hands stay at the waist. The whole lesson is that attention arrives only when she is not jumping.

### 11. plan-mat — asleep on the mat
> Lucy lies **flat on her side, fully asleep** on a soft mat — legs extended, body
> relaxed, eyes closed. Nearby, a person sits quietly on a chair or sofa doing
> something of their own, paying her no attention at all. Soft, domestic, late-afternoon
> light. Deep quiet.

**Must be true:** she is genuinely asleep and flat out, and the person is not interacting with her.

## Notes for the generator

Ask for one scene per image, at 1448×1086. If a scene comes back with the guest in
a plaid shirt, with a taut leash where the prompt says slack, or with the handler
bending over the dog from above, it is worth regenerating — those three are the
errors that change what the picture teaches.

The twelve images from 19 August are already correct and app-ready; they cover the
doorbell, the knock, the guest arriving and leaving, the greeting, the jump, and
the seated visit.
